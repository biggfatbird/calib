Unit of analysis: one dataset. n = 8.
Outcome: mean paired Brier difference at sigma = 1, recalibrated arm minus baseline. The mean is the mean over 50 draws of the five-split average. The draw interval in the effect-size figure is the 2.5 and 97.5 percent linear quantile of those 50 numbers. It is not a confidence interval for a new dataset, and the 50 draws are not the sample size of the Spearman correlation.
Characteristics: rows, features, smaller-class rate, baseline 10-bin ECE at sigma 0, baseline ROC-AUC at sigma 0, and the increase in baseline Brier from sigma 0 to sigma 1. The last three are computed from the same experiment as the outcome. They are descriptions, not exogenous causes, and not a prediction model.
Association: Spearman rho. Uncertainty: percentile interval from 4000 bootstrap resamples of the 8 datasets. Leave-one-out rho range is stored beside each correlation. No line is fit to the scatters.
Spearman family: 6 existing Brier contrasts (sigmoid and isotonic minus baseline, for logistic regression, random forest, and RBF SVM) crossed with 6 characteristics. 36 two-sided permutation tests. Holm is applied inside this family only. The dataset is the unit (n=8).
Permutation p-values permute the Brier difference across the 8 datasets, 4999 times, two-sided, with the add-one correction (1 + hits) / (4999 + 1). Holm adjustment uses those 36 p-values only.
Two Holm families, adjusted separately. Cross-dataset: the 64 pre-specified Nadeau-Bengio tests (logistic isotonic Brier, logistic isotonic ROC-AUC, logistic sigmoid Brier, forest sigmoid Brier; sigma 0 and 1; eight datasets). WDBC: the 48 Brier and 10-bin ECE tests (sigmoid and isotonic minus baseline; three models; four sigmas).
Holm is the step-down procedure: sort p-values, multiply rank i by (m - i + 1), cap at 1, then enforce nondecreasing adjusted values. Raw files under results/recalibration/ and results/cross_dataset/ are not rewritten.
This analysis does not claim that a characteristic predicts recalibration benefit.
